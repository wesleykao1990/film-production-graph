# Story Room Gate — Rater Guide

You will see randomized outputs for the same story brief. Do not guess which system produced them. Judge only the text shown.

## Specificity / non-substitutability

Prefer the sample whose setting, profession, objects, social rules, and relationship history materially cause what happens. A sample is weaker when names and location could be swapped without changing the scene.

## Character voice

Prefer the sample in which characters sound and behave differently because of their goals, tactics, history, vocabulary, rhythm, omissions, and relationship. Do not reward ornate prose by itself.

## Causal dramatic progression

Prefer the sample in which a character's choice under pressure causes a turn and changes power, knowledge, commitment, or relationship state. Do not reward a sequence that is merely busy or surprising.

## Ties on scored comparisons

For ordinary A/B/C comparisons, choose tie when neither sample is meaningfully stronger on the named dimension. Do not use tie merely because both are imperfect.

## Positive-control anchors

Anchor questions are forced choice and deliberately contain a large known contrast. Select the sample that is stronger on the named dimension; no tie option is shown. If a sample is missing, unreadable, truncated, or otherwise technically defective, use **Report technical problem**. The task must be replaced before the dataset is frozen; do not guess and do not convert the problem into a tie.

## Pilot

Before the final run, at least two non-builder raters complete one non-test calibration triplet, explain the dimensions in their own words, and report elapsed time. The final assignment should take no more than 45 minutes. If the pilot is too long, recruit the seventh rater or shorten the rated scene within the predeclared range; do not reduce the number of raters assigned to each triplet.
