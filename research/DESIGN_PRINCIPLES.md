# Collected Design Principles

These principles summarize the architecture lessons that motivated the package.

1. **A hierarchical story process is necessary but not sufficient.** A generic premise can be polished into a generic screenplay. The upstream process needs specific evidence, contradiction, relationship leverage, and explicit forbidden defaults.
2. **Character descriptions are not character agency.** Characters need local knowledge, relationship state, objectives, tactics, self-image, and permission to hesitate, evade, misread, or fail.
3. **Continuity should be symbolic before it is visual.** Explicit world, dramatic, and audience state outranks prompt memory and reference-image similarity.
4. **Prompts are compilation outputs.** They translate locked intent into provider syntax; they do not author new facts.
5. **Local repair preserves good decisions.** A finding should name the violated rule, path, evidence, and permitted patch scope.
6. **The soundtrack is persistent.** Voice identity, performances, music cues, ambience, Foley, and effects should not be regenerated independently with every picture shot.
7. **Independent generation needs a continuous timeline.** Shots may be generated separately; sequence pacing, audio, and editorial meaning are not separate.
8. **Rejected work is valuable lineage.** The creator's selection and rejection reasons become the system's taste memory.
9. **Multiple agents are useful only when their authority, evidence, state, outputs, and incentives differ.** Persona labels around the same context are not meaningful diversity.
10. **Evaluation dimensions should remain separate.** Technical polish must not compensate for weak originality, character specificity, or causal agency.
11. **Human approval should occur at high-leverage points.** Premise selection, Scene Contract, screenplay lock, animatic approval, asset select, and release are more important than approving every minor deterministic transform.
12. **Provider independence protects the project.** The story and production graph should outlive any one model, API, or proprietary canvas.
13. **Custom skills are software dependencies even when they contain only instructions.** They require versioning, tests, permissions, provenance, and explicit upgrades.
14. **Build the cheapest coherent representation first.** A broken animatic should not be rendered as expensive final video.
